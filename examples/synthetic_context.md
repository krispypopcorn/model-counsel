# Synthetic Contract-Supply Research Mandate

This example is fictional. Northstar Supply Studio is a hypothetical small business
used to demonstrate the workflow without publishing a real person's finances,
employment history, contacts, or commercial decisions.

## Objective

Evaluate evidence-backed opportunities to supply repeat-purchase physical products to
institutional and mid-market buyers in Canada. Begin with procurement records and buyer
requirements, then identify three opportunities worthy of direct validation. A recommendation
is permission to run a low-cost test, not permission to purchase inventory or sign a contract.

## Operator profile

The fictional operator has experience translating technical requirements into commercial
proposals, coordinating vendors, analyzing tabular data, and managing multi-stakeholder sales.
The operator can perform light quality-assurance coordination but does not own a factory,
warehouse, or vehicle fleet.

## Economic and risk assumptions

- Treat CAD 10,000–100,000 as a validation-order range, CAD 50,000–300,000 as a
  reference-building range, and CAD 250,000–2 million as a possible scale-stage range.
- Explore a path to approximately CAD 200,000 in annual gross profit and CAD 100,000 in
  owner income; these are synthetic scenario variables, not forecasts.
- Prefer deposits, direct shipment, purchase-order financing, or supplier terms over
  speculative inventory.
- Never infer committed revenue from a framework ceiling, open tender, estimated quantity,
  market-size report, or unsigned letter of intent.
- Stop when mandatory qualifications, concentration, product liability, rejected-shipment
  exposure, or peak working capital exceed the capabilities of a small entrant.

## Evidence standard

Prefer official tenders, award notices, contract-history records, buyer specifications,
regulatory sources, written supplier quotations, and buyer interviews. Every material claim
must be labelled as a verified fact, evidence-backed estimate, working assumption, or unknown.
Shortlisted opportunities should have at least two identifiable purchasing events from the
last 36 months or be explicitly marked as insufficiently supported.

## Decision criteria

Assess purchasing recurrence, accessible contract size, buyer reachability, qualification
burden, incumbent strength, supplier redundancy, quality inspectability, gross-profit
potential, cash conversion, founder workload, concentration, regulatory exposure, and the
cost of the next real-world validation step. Use score ranges and preserve disagreements.

## Required outcome

Produce an opportunity map, three candidate validations, a comparison, a conservative risk
audit, and a final decision report. The final report must include evidence gaps, a rejection
log, a 30/60/90-day validation plan, and explicit proceed/revise/stop gates before material
spending or contractual commitments.
